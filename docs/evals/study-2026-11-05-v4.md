The unifying word is εἷς (heis), "one," masculine singular, so the sense is "you are all one" — one man, one new man in Christ [Lexicon][JFB].

**Life in biblical times.** Under the law the male sex had particular privileges: males alone bore circumcision, the sign of the covenant, while baptism is applied to male and female alike; males alone could be kings and priests, whereas all of either sex are now kings and priests unto God; and males had prior right to inheritances [JFB]. Among the Jews there was also a disparity in religious privileges between men and women, who in many cases were treated more like children than adults [Clarke].

**Theological significance.** The verse teaches that sonship by faith in Christ leaves no class privileged above another, as the Jews had been above the Gentiles under the law [JFB]. Christ belongs alike to slave and free by faith [JFB]. Difference of sex makes no difference in Christian privileges [JFB]. All who believe become one family through Christ, one body of which He is the head [Clarke]. The text states unity and equal welcome; how far that unity reshapes specific roles and structures is where Christians have read the passage differently, and the sources here do not settle that question.

**Related passages.** 1 Corinthians 12:12–13 speaks of many parts forming one body, baptized into one body whether Jews or Greeks, slave or free [Cross-ref]. Colossians 3:11 says there is no Greek or Jew, slave or free, but Christ is all and is in all [Cross-ref]. Romans 10:12–15 states there is no difference between Jew and Greek, since the same Lord is Lord of all [Cross-ref].
